# The switch: the store opens document handles, and the transport goes

**Status**: In Progress
**Date**: 2026-08-28

## What this is

The one commit ADR-0277 needs, written down before it is attempted because it
cannot be attempted twice. Everything around it is built and green: the
authority that reads, the replica, the wire, the session, the hub, the handle,
alarm-driven folding, and the hide flush. 422 tests pass and no shipping file
has changed behaviour. What remains is removing the old transport, and it is
one commit because every seam between the two is a file both halves import.

Decisions are in ADR-0276 through ADR-0279. Nothing here re-decides anything;
this is the order of operations and the oracle.

## Why it cannot be split

Every candidate intermediate was tried on paper and fails at the same joint.
`DurablePort`'s `load()` returns `{ updates, outbox: OutboxEntry[], cursor,
lastId }` — a chain of appends, a queue of unsent ones addressed by id, and a
log position. **That contract is the log.** Backing it with whole-document
blobs means re-implementing the log inside the port, which is more code than
either end and throws all of it away one commit later.

So the port does not move to a new medium. It stops existing.

## The oracle

The claim the whole plan rests on: **the typed surface does not move.**

- **99 tests never mention a port, cursor, outbox or envelope.** invariants 18,
  document-authority 15, blobs 15, deletion-model 9, document 7,
  flush-on-hide 7, delta-names-the-row 7, validation 7, document-frames 6,
  what-the-crdt-buys 5, authority 2, independent-document-roots 1.
- **`store.test.ts` has 61 tests and 6 transport references**, all in its setup
  (`createSqliteDurablePort` to build a store, `encodeEnvelope` to deliver
  remote bytes) plus one test about the outbox.

**≈158 tests must come back green with only `store.test.ts`'s setup changed.**
If they do, the surface did not move; if one fails on anything but setup, stop
and read it rather than adjusting it.

## Order of operations

Each step compiles. Only the last is expected to go red, and only in the files
being deleted.

1. **`openDatabase` takes `Blobs` and opens a root handle.** `createLocalStore`
   and `createAccountStore` stop taking a `DurablePort`. The root handle's key
   is `app`; row document keys are `<table>/<rowId>`, both under the generation
   segment (ADR-0276).
2. **`documents.ts` becomes a map of handles.** `open(address)` returns a
   handle from the map or opens one; `close` removes it. Delete `acceptRemote`,
   `retire`'s durable half, and `states()` — a handle persists itself and there
   is no batch to join.
3. **Row deletion retires without a tombstone table.** `deleteRow` removes the
   scalar row and `blobs.remove(key)` for its document. `_tombstones` and
   `DocumentRetired` go: what they prevented — a derive re-minting a deleted
   row — is unreachable since `updateRow` cannot mint (074d1deb).
4. **`store.ts` loses its transport half.** `ClientLog`, `SyncEngine`,
   `syncEngineOf`, `SyncCapability`, `createClientLog`, and `persistence` off
   `DataStoreBase`. `stateVector`/`encodeStateSince` move to the handle.
5. **`browser.ts` and `memory.ts` take `Blobs`.** browser hands in
   `createOpfsBlobs`; memory hands in one over a `Map`, which is the first
   in-memory `Blobs` and is justified here rather than in the library because
   `openMemory` has real consumers (`packages/chat`, `packages/skills`, four
   artifact and app tests) and none of them want a filesystem.
6. **`attach.ts` dials per document.** One socket per open handle, at
   `/v1/data/<dataId>/generations/<n>/<key>`.
7. **Delete.** `persistence.ts`, `log.ts`, `client.ts`, `connection.ts`,
   `hub.ts`, `authority.ts`, `frames.ts`, `envelope.ts`, plus
   `port-conformance.test.ts`, `transport.test.ts`, `connection.test.ts`,
   `persistence.test.ts`, `sync.test.ts`, `attach.test.ts`,
   `document-identity.test.ts`, `retention.test.ts`. Drop the `document-`
   prefix from the five new sync files, because the old names are free.

## Hazards, in the order they will bite

- **A sync bug does not throw.** It under-sends, and the other device is
  quietly missing a paragraph. The ordering rule is the guard: bytes durable
  before anything says they were sent. `document-handle.ts`'s `settle()` has it
  in the right order; nothing may reorder it for latency.
- **`store.test.ts:935`** asserts "local commits leave the outbox empty and no
  replica verb exists". It tests a mechanism rather than a behaviour, and what
  it protected — a device store must never grow work it owes nobody — is
  unreachable when there is no outbox. **Delete it rather than translate it.**
- **`deriveOnCommit` fires on a row document's commit.** Under handles it must
  still reach the root handle's document, which is a different handle. This is
  the one cross-handle call in the design and the place to look first if a
  derived title stops updating.
- **`claims.ts` locks a store address.** Under one blob store per database that
  is unchanged, but a handle does not take its own lock: two handles on one
  document are the same handle, from the map.

## After it lands

Wave B: the mirror takes `Blobs` and a table subscription rather than the
database's private surface, and `stored()`/`onCommitted()` leave
`DataStoreBase`, which reaches one member.

Wave C: `view` and `store` stop being two objects, because the measurement that
justified them ("thirteen names where four are used") expires with the
transport. Then `store` → `database`, once, over a surface that has stopped
moving.

Delete this spec when the switch lands. The decisions are already ADRs; this is
scaffolding.
